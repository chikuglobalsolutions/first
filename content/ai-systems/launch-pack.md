### Post 3 — How-to value
A simple follow-up sequence that recovers booked appointments you're currently losing:

1. **Minute 1** — Instant reply. "Hi [Name], got your request — are you looking for [service] this week or next?" Speed matters more than polish.

2. **Hour 1** — If no reply, send a booking link with two concrete time options. Choices convert better than "let me know."

3. **Day 1** — A short value message. One tip, one reassurance, no pressure.

4. **Day 3** — The "did life get busy?" nudge. This one recovers more bookings than people expect.

5. **Day 7** — Last touch. "Want me to hold a spot or close this out?" Permission to say no gets you a yes surprisingly often.

You can run this by hand. Most owners won't, because they're busy running the business. That's exactly why we automate it — but the sequence works whether a human or a system sends it.

### Post 6 — How-to value
How to know if you're quietly losing leads (a 5-minute audit you can do today):

1. Fill out your own contact form. Time how long until you get a reply. Over 5 minutes? You're losing people.

2. Call your own main line during a busy hour. Does it go to voicemail? Does anyone text you back?

3. Look at your last 20 leads. How many got more than one follow-up? Be honest.

4. Count your Google reviews from the last 90 days. Fewer than one a week means you're leaving trust on the table.

5. Check what happens after someone books. Do they get a reminder? No-shows are usually a reminder problem, not a patient problem.

If two or more of those made you wince, you don't have a marketing problem. You have a systems problem — and that's fixable in about 30 days.
